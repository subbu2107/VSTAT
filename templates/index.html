How does valuation work?

There are two methods to calculate the Intrinsic Value of a stock: DCF Valuation and Relative Valuation. We take the average of these two methods to estimate the intrinsic value as accurately as possible.

What is DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)?

Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) is a valuation method used to estimate the intrinsic value of an investment, business, or asset based on its expected future cash flows. It works by projecting how much cash the asset will generate in the future and then discounting those cash flows back to their present value using a discount rate.

In VSTAT, intrinsic value calculation uses the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) method. For most companies, it uses a Free Cash Flow (FCF)-based approach, where past FCF data is collected, averaged, and projected into the future with estimated growth rates. Future FCFs are then discounted using WACC to determine present value.

What is Relative Valuation?

In VSTAT, relative valuation compares a stock's valuation multiples (P/E and P/B) with its own historical averages and those of sector peers. The system retrieves EPS and BVPS to calculate average ratios and estimates fair value by combining multiple valuation approaches.
